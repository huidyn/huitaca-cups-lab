# Çay glass: tile-inspired pattern studies

The supplied middle row proposes eight-point stars, a diamond lattice and
overlapping arches/scales. Alessandro retained the first two and chose
Çintamani instead of scales, keeping this collection distinct from the
Japanese wave pattern.

## Vessel and palette

The original glass remains labelled Türkiye / Azerbaijan, following
[UNESCO's shared çay culture entry](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/culture-of-ay-tea-a-symbol-of-identity-hospitality-and-social-interaction-01685).
The decorated collection is Türkiye / Çay glass because its principal tile
references are Anatolian Seljuk and Ottoman. It is not presented as a
universal Islamic vessel or an authenticated historical tea-glass decoration.
The tiles and the modern drinking-glass form are separate references.

The palette follows the illustration: turquoise `#2fa3a0`, cream `#f6f4ee`,
and a gold `#c9a24b` rim. Gold is a flat colour, not a metallic shader.
The inside is darker turquoise; glass remains opaque and unlit like the
other avatars. The illustration's cobalt is not needed for this first set.

## Eight-point stars / Sekiz köşeli yıldızlar

[Archnet's Kubadabad Palace record](https://www.archnet.org/sites/4018)
documents white eight-point star tiles against turquoise cruciform tiles in
Anatolian Seljuk palace decoration. Many contain figures and royal imagery.
The lab borrows the broad star-shaped geometric idea, not those figures,
inscriptions or emblems. Its newly drawn star outlines have eight large tips
and open centres, connected between two framing rules.

## Diamond lattice / Baklava örgüsü

The supplied glass guides the large open diamond chain and framing rules.
[The Met's geometric-pattern essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/geometric-patterns-in-islamic-art)
documents square-based, repeated and interlaced geometric constructions.
This supports the formal family, not a claim that this exact simple band
has one exclusive Turkish origin or historical name. The Turkish label
describes the shape rather than asserting a named traditional design.

## Çintamani / Üç benek ve dalgalı çizgiler

- [The Met, İznik tile 2019.173, ca. 1560–70](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/823310)
  documents clusters of three roundels accompanied by wavy stripes.
  [The exact photograph, DP-19634-001](https://images.metmuseum.org/CRDImages/is/original/DP-19634-001.jpg)
  shows upright/inverted dot triangles separated by pairs of long, tapered
  diagonal stripes. This photograph, not merely the catalogue description,
  governs the lab's layout.
- [V&A, Ottoman tile 425-1900, 1560–90](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O113294/tile-unknown/)
  documents three dots and paired wavy lines on tilework.

The museums give differing accounts of the motif's distant origins: The Met
connects it with Buddhist jewels, while the V&A stresses animal-pelt designs
and disputes that connection. The lab identifies the Ottoman visual structure
without resolving that disagreement or assigning a universal sacred meaning.

Four three-roundel groups alternate upright and inverted around the glass,
with paired tapered diagonals between them. The earlier horizontal
side-by-side squiggles are removed. The small coloured centres visible in
the photograph are omitted, retaining large, flat cream dots for avatars.
This spacing is a modern composition choice, not a prescribed repeat count.

## Rendering and adaptation

Eight stars or eight diamonds wrap around the curved lower wall. Çintamani
uses four groups, with an inverted front-facing cluster and a thumbnail showing
the roundels and diagonal separators. Drawings repeat across the texture seam and remain one cream
ink on the turquoise body.

Only the decorated variant raises the face to Y=1.22. Its band occupies
Y=0.30–0.88, leaving 0.165 units below the eyes. Grey Shapes geometry, face,
colour slots, saucer and previews stay unchanged. The existing channel-mask
shader, on-demand rendering, 25% resolution margin and 2048-pixel cap are
reused; no additional meshes, lights, shadows or passes are introduced.
Research photographs are not redistributed.

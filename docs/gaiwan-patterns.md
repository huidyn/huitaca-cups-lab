# Gaiwan: decorative pattern study

The supplied reference proposes cloud scrolls, lotus petals and an angular
fret. These three choices are not an exhaustive catalogue of Chinese decoration.
The lab keeps their recognizable structures, not every line or the reference's
repeat counts. The blue-on-ivory scheme is shared by all three and remains
independent of the mask.

## Cloud scrolls, yunwen 云纹

- [Legle's Auspicious Clouds Ruyi Gaiwan](https://legleporcelain.com/product/ruyi-auspicious-cloud-gaiwan/)
  provides an actual gaiwan example with broad lobed, curling cloud forms.
- [Gotheborg's ruyi glossary](https://www.gotheborg.com/glossary/ruyi.shtml)
  describes the cloud-shaped or heart-shaped head used alone or repeated as a
  porcelain border. Ruyi and cloud motifs overlap, but are not identical names
  for every curled form.
- Simplification: large lobes, one inward curl, a flowing connection. No
  ceremonial scepter, imperial insignia or exact historical reproduction.

## Lotus petals, lianban 莲瓣

- [British Museum PDF B.658](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_PDF-B-658)
  describes overlapping lotus panels rising from a bowl's foot.
- [Wing Hop Fung's blue-and-white lotus gaiwan](https://winghopfung.com/products/blue-and-white-intertwined-lotus-gaiwan)
  shows lotus flowers and winding stems on a modern gaiwan. This illustrates
  lotus use on gaiwans, not the exact fan in the supplied reference.
- [Gotheborg's lotus glossary](https://www.gotheborg.com/glossary/lotus.shtml)
  explains its associations with purity and its Buddhist and Daoist contexts.
- Simplification: five broad pointed petals in a fan, without veins, small
  stamens or a vine. This is a decorative petal motif, not an Eight Buddhist
  Emblems set or a claim about the petal count of a sacred lotus.

## Fret border, huiwen 回纹

- [British Museum PDF A.421](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_PDF-A-421)
  documents an incised key-fret border and lotus panels on a Chinese bowl.
- [Gotheborg's meander glossary](https://www.gotheborg.com/glossary/meander.shtml)
  includes a ceramic example and drawings of angular and curved variants.
- Simplification: six large repeats with thick right-angle turns and a single
  inward return per unit, leaving open gaps at small sizes. Do not equate
  every later ceramic fret with the exact meaning or layout of an ancient
  bronze leiwen ground, or label the Chinese version as exclusively Greek.

## What the sources do and do not establish

These sources show varied decorative treatments. None consulted prescribes a
universal repeat count or requires fine detail for these everyday ceramic
borders. That supports a clearly labelled contemporary simplification, not a
claim that all Chinese religious or ceremonial imagery can be freely altered.
The lab does not add sacred inscriptions, dynastic marks or named ritual sets.
Online photographs are research references only; the SVG drawings are new,
simplified constructions and the photographs are not redistributed here.
